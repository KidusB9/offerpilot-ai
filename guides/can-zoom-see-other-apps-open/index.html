Can Zoom see what other apps you have open?

2026 · ~6 min read · OfferPilot AI

No. A Zoom meeting is a stream of audio, video, chat and whatever screen frames you deliberately hand over, and there is no field in that stream for a list of your running programs. The host's panel contains no process list, no installed-software view and no button that asks for one. Three things genuinely can expose your other apps, and none of them is the Zoom call itself: anything you choose to share, a separate proctoring client an employer requires you to install, and, historically, an attention indicator that Zoom deleted in 2020 rather than making quieter.

Why the client on your desk has nothing to send

There are two Zooms, and the answer is the same for both for different reasons.

The web client is a page in a browser tab. A web page has no way to list installed software or running processes, because no such browser API exists to call. It gets a camera stream, a microphone stream and, if you allow it, one screen or window or tab. The picker in that flow is drawn by Chrome or Edge, not by Zoom, and the page is handed exactly one source out of it. Everything else is on the far side of the sandbox wall.

The desktop client is an ordinary user-space application. It is not a kernel driver, not a system service and not an endpoint monitoring agent, and it installs none of those. It has one moment where it looks at your windows: when you click Share Screen, it builds the tile grid from your open top-level windows so you have something to choose from. That grid is rendered on your own computer. Open it, read every tile, press Escape, and none of it has crossed the network.

What the other side receives is short and dull: your display name, join and leave times, mute and video state, reactions, chat you type, and the pixels of a share once you start one. On paid accounts an account administrator also gets quality dashboards showing client version, operating system and connection statistics. That is metadata about the Zoom client itself, not a list of what else is running, and your interviewer is not sitting in an admin dashboard anyway.

Read those rows in order and the shape of the risk is obvious. It is not the video call. It is the frame you chose to send and the software you were asked to install. Sharing Entire Screen is the commonest way people show an interviewer their whole application list, and they do it voluntarily while worrying about something Zoom cannot do.

The proctoring exception deserves no softening. If a company asks you to install a client before a timed assessment, that client does the thing Zoom does not: reads what is running, often refuses to start until you close things, sometimes watches for new processes mid-test. The consent screen says so. Read it, because that, and not the meeting app, is the accurate description of what is watching you.

Attention tracking was deleted, not made invisible

This is where most of the anxiety comes from, and the feature it refers to has not existed for years.

Zoom's attendee attention status worked like this. A host could enable it. While that host was screen sharing, if a participant's Zoom window sat out of focus for more than thirty seconds, a small clock indicator appeared beside that participant and the meeting report carried a column for it. That was the whole feature. It never reported which application had taken focus, it did not run when nobody was sharing, and it did nothing if the host left it switched off.

Zoom removed it in April 2020, during the ninety-day security push that followed a wave of privacy criticism. Removed is the operative word. The host setting is gone from the account controls, the indicator is gone from the participant list, the column is gone from the reports, and nothing quieter took its place.

A web page can know that it lost focus. It cannot know what won it.

That distinction is why the myth outlived the feature. Tab-switch and focus detection are genuinely real, but they live on browser-based assessment platforms, not in Zoom. A page can be notified by the browser that it was backgrounded or that its window lost focus, so a coding assessment can log the event and count it. It still cannot see what you switched to. "Candidate left the tab four times" is a claim those platforms can make. "Candidate opened a chatbot" is not.

Ninety seconds that settle it for good

Start a meeting with yourself

Open a meeting on your PC and join it from your phone as a second participant. Mute both to avoid feedback. The phone now stands in for the interviewer's eyes.

Open the share picker and just look

Click Share Screen. Every open window sits there as a labelled tile. This is the enumeration people fear, happening on your own machine so you have something to pick. Watch the phone: nothing changes.

Press Escape

Nothing was selected, encoded or sent. That is the whole difference between an application knowing something locally and a meeting transmitting it.

Share a single window, then Alt-Tab away

Share one document window, then switch to your browser or your notes. The phone keeps showing the shared window. The app you switched to never appears, and nothing marks that you switched.

Now share Entire Screen and repeat

Same actions again. This time the phone shows the taskbar, the Alt-Tab strip, the toast that arrives mid-demo and every tray icon. Same meeting, same client, entirely different exposure, decided by which tile you clicked.

It answers the question for your build of Zoom rather than the one somebody wrote about three years ago.

Where we would be overstating our own product

Since this question often arrives attached to ours, here is the boundary drawn honestly. The OfferPilot AI overlay is excluded from screen capture at the operating system level, so it is absent from a Zoom share, from Teams, Meet and OBS, and from screenshots, while staying visible on your own monitor, on every plan including the free tier.

Now the over-read we would rather correct than exploit. Capture exclusion is about frames, not about process lists. Anything that enumerates running software sees a process whether or not it appears in a screen capture, so a required proctoring client, or the management agent on a company laptop, is not affected by capture exclusion in the slightest. It also does nothing about a webcam, a lockdown browser, a proctor in the room, eye tracking, or a phone camera pointed at your desk. On a monitored assessment with a required proctoring client it is the wrong tool, and we would rather say so than sell you a subscription. It is Windows 10 and 11 only, so none of it exists on a Mac. To check the capture behaviour rather than take our word, the free tier on the pricing page needs no card.

The short version

  • A Zoom call has no process list to send. No host control displays one, and the web client is sandboxed away from even asking.
  • The share picker is local. Escape sends nothing.
  • Entire Screen is the real leak. Your taskbar and toasts are the app list, in picture form, sent voluntarily.
  • Proctoring clients and managed laptops do enumerate. That is their job, it is disclosed, and it is a different tool from the meeting.
  • Attention tracking is gone, not hidden. Removed in April 2020, never replaced, and it never named an app even when it worked.
