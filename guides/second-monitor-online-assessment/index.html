Can you use two monitors during an online assessment?

2026 · ~7 min read · OfferPilot AI

Sometimes, and the answer is written down in the one place candidates skip: the rules panel you click through before the timer starts. An unproctored employer test sent as an emailed link almost never mentions displays. A proctored assessment, a lockdown client or a remote certification exam usually requires exactly one, in plain language, and some refuse to launch until the count drops to one. Where a rule exists it binds you, whether or not anything technical enforces it.

Four regimes, and which one your invite belongs to

Almost every online assessment falls into one of four buckets, and the display question has a different answer in each. Work out your bucket first, because it decides everything below.

  • Unproctored employer test. A link, a deadline of a few days, no camera step, no download. Nothing watches your hardware. Displays are not mentioned because nobody is looking.
  • Browser proctored assessment. Runs in a normal tab but asks for camera, sometimes microphone, and often a share of your entire screen. It records and flags rather than blocks. Display rules here are usually stated as instructions rather than enforced as a gate.
  • Lockdown client or remote exam. You install and launch a dedicated application. It owns the machine for the duration, and the single display requirement is normally hard, checked at launch, and paired with a room scan.
  • Live human interview. A person on a call. There is no enumeration and no rule engine, only what the interviewer infers from your eyes and your pointer.

What display enumeration actually lets software see

This is where guesswork usually replaces fact, so here is the real capability ladder, from a plain web page up to an installed client.

  • A web page, no permission. In Chromium browsers, screen.isExtended is a boolean readable without any prompt: true means more than one display is attached. That is all it reveals, with no sizes, no names, no contents. Firefox and Safari do not implement it, where it reads as undefined.
  • A web page, permission granted. getScreenDetails() sits behind an explicit window management permission prompt. Grant it and the page receives an array covering every display: width and height, position within the virtual desktop, which one is primary, which is the built in laptop panel, device pixel ratio, and a label.
  • Positional inference, always available. window.screenX and window.screenY are coordinates in the virtual desktop. A negative X, or an X larger than screen.width, means the window sits on a panel that is not the primary one. No permission, no prompt, just arithmetic.
  • The screen share stream. A capture handed over by getDisplayMedia carries one surface and one only. Its settings include displaySurface, which reads monitor, window or browser, so a proctored page can tell whether you shared a whole display or quietly picked a single tab. It cannot see the panel you did not pick.
  • An installed client. Full operating system enumeration: the count, every panel's resolution and coordinates, the primary flag, adapter and monitor names. Windows also broadcasts a display change message, so plugging a monitor in mid session is a timestamped event rather than a quiet one. Given capture rights, such a client can record all of them at once.

Two arrangements confuse the count, and neither is a loophole worth building a plan on. Mirrored displays are one desktop painted twice, so enumeration returns one. A spanned mode such as NVIDIA Surround presents several panels to Windows as one enormous logical display, which reports as a single screen at an implausible resolution. In both cases the panels are still lit on your desk, a webcam or a room scan sees them, and under mirroring everything on the spare panel is already inside the shared stream anyway.

A five minute check, run before the test and not during it

Search the invite for the actual words

Open the invite email and the assessment landing page, then use Ctrl and F for monitor, display, screen and webcam. Most of the time the rule is sitting in a bulleted list you scrolled past. If it says one display, stop here, the question is answered.

Ask in writing if it is silent

One line to the recruiter: "Is a second monitor permitted during the assessment, or should I disconnect it beforehand?" A written yes costs nothing and settles it permanently. A non answer is itself informative, and you default to one display.

See what a page can learn about your setup

On any ordinary web page in Chrome or Edge, press F12, open Console, and run screen.isExtended, then screen.width, then window.screenX. True on the first means a page can tell you have more than one display without asking you anything. A screenX of minus 1920 means the browser window is on the panel to the left of your primary.

Set the configuration deliberately

If the rule is one display, unplug the cable or power the second panel off rather than merely ignoring it, and do it before launching anything. If two are allowed, press Windows key and P and confirm Extend, then in Settings, System, Display click Identify and make the panel you will work on the main display, so notification toasts land where you expect them.

Run the system check on the finished setup

Most proctored platforms ship a pre test system check. Run it after you have arranged the desk, not before, so it validates the real configuration. If the platform has no check, start a solo meeting, share your entire screen and watch it from your phone for thirty seconds.

What goes wrong even when two monitors are permitted

Permission is not the same as painless. Four recurring problems, none of which involves a display count at all:

  • Full screen enforcement counts focus, not panels. An assessment pushed into full screen logs every exit with a timestamp and a duration. Clicking documentation on your second monitor produces exactly the same log line as alt tabbing on a single screen.
  • The camera reads your eyes, not your hardware. Long sideways gaze at something outside the frame is the most commonly reported trigger for human review, and a second monitor is the most common reason for it.
  • The share picker sends the wrong panel. Screen 1 in a picker is that application's own numbering, not the number Windows shows you. Choose by the live thumbnail, then confirm by the coloured border lighting up around the physical panel you meant.
  • Windows and dialogs open where you cannot see them. An assessment IDE that remembers a position from last week can open a file dialog on the panel your camera is not framing, and you lose forty seconds of a timed test hunting for it.

The short version

  • Your bucket decides it. Unproctored test, no rule. Lockdown client or certification exam, one display, usually enforced at launch.
  • A plain web page learns a boolean, an installed client learns everything. Count, sizes, positions, primary flag and hot plug events all come from ordinary system calls.
  • Unverified is not permission. Two minutes with Ctrl and F on the invite beats any general article, this one included.
