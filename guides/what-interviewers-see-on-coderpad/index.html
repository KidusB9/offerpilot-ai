Can CoderPad see if you switch tabs, and what can HackerRank see?

2026 · ~7 min read · OfferPilot AI

Both can see that the editor stopped being the focused page, which is what a tab switch looks like from inside a browser. The difference is delivery. CoderPad surfaces it to a human watching the same document while you are still away, and keeps it on the playback timeline. HackerRank turns it into a timestamped row in a report somebody opens later. Neither learns where you went, and neither records your camera or your screen unless the employer enabled that option and your browser asked your permission first.

Most answers online mix four different products together. A live CoderPad pad, a CoderPad Screen take home, a standard HackerRank test and a HackerRank test with proctoring switched on collect different things and hand them to different people. This page separates them by capability, so you can match the round you are actually sitting.

Work out which mode you are in before you type a line

Nearly every setting in that table is identifiable from what your own screen does in the first two minutes. The sequence below takes about ninety seconds.

Read the instructions page, not the problem

Proctored HackerRank tests state it before the timer starts, and the wording tells you which options are live. Full screen required, camera required and screen share required are three separate sentences, and a test can carry any combination of them.

Count the browser permission prompts

Camera access draws a dialog your browser owns, and on most laptops the indicator light comes on and stays on. Screen capture draws the picker listing Entire Screen, Window and Tab, then leaves a sharing indicator up. A website cannot restyle, suppress or auto accept either one. No prompt means no pixels.

Watch what happens the moment the test starts

If the tab pushes itself into full screen, exits are being counted. Leave full screen deliberately once at the very start, while nothing is at stake, and read the warning it gives you. Now you know the exact behaviour before it matters.

Test the paste behaviour with something harmless

Paste one comment line from outside, your own name will do, and see whether the editor marks it. On a live pad, say what you are doing first. You then know whether your language template will arrive flagged, which is worth knowing before you paste it under time pressure.

Ask the recruiter, in writing

Is the assessment proctored, and is a camera required, is a fair question with a short answer. Asking it early also settles any accommodation you need, which is far simpler before the link is issued than after a flag exists.

The same ninety second absence, in four rounds

One behaviour, four different artefacts. This is why a single answer to the tab switching question never fits every case.

  1. Live CoderPad pad. The interviewer gets a notice while you are gone and often asks whether everything is alright when you come back. It also leaves a marker on the replay, sitting beside whatever code appeared afterwards.
  2. CoderPad Screen take home. Nobody is watching in the moment. The gap sits on the timeline, and a reviewer scrubs to it only if the finished code already looked unusual for the time taken.
  3. Standard HackerRank test, no proctoring options. A focus event and the timing pattern across your submissions. No camera, no screen capture, no live nudge, and nothing you will be told about.
  4. Proctored HackerRank test. A row in the activity log, a full screen exit if you left it, and, where the employer enabled the camera, snapshots covering the same minutes.

In all four, duration matters less than adjacency. Twelve short losses spread across an hour reads as notifications left switched on. One long gap immediately before forty finished lines that pass first time reads as a story, even though the record itself never says where you were.

Every flag either platform produces has the same shape. Focus left, then this appeared. Never: you were here.

Where OfferPilot AI is the wrong tool

Worth stating plainly on a page published by a company that sells an interview assistant. Our overlay is excluded from screen capture at the operating system level, so it does not show up in a Zoom, Teams or Meet share, in a recording or in a screenshot. That property does nothing for anything in the table above. Focus detection never looks at pixels, it reads which window the operating system considers focused, so clicking any overlay, or firing the Code Copilot capture shortcut, moves focus off the editor and produces exactly the marker anything else would. Capture exclusion is also no answer to webcam monitoring, a lockdown browser, eye tracking, a second device camera or a person sitting in the room with you.

There is a second reason to leave it closed here. Both platforms record how the answer was built, and a live pad adds an interviewer who will ask you to change that code in front of them. A solution you cannot rebuild, defend and extend under questioning reads worse than an honest partial one in your own hand. The useful place for an assistant is the preparation beforehand, and the conversational rounds where you are talking rather than typing into a monitored editor.

A short checklist for either platform

  • Keep the editor on one screen and put nothing you will click on the other, because clicking a second monitor blurs the page while it stays fully visible to you.
  • Narrate anything that moves focus on a live pad, the developer console included. Opening dev tools registers as leaving, docked or undocked.
  • Type your scaffolding rather than pasting it. Typed text replays as typing, while the same text pasted from a notes app arrives in one frame and gets marked.
  • Run your code early and often. Failing runs followed by passing ones are the clearest evidence on the timeline that real debugging happened.
  • Silence notifications before you start, since every toast that steals focus becomes an event someone may scrub to later.
