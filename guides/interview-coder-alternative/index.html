Interview Coder Alternative: Multi-Scroll Screenshot Capture on Windows

2026 · ~6 min read · OfferPilot AI

If you are shopping past Interview Coder, the thing to compare is not answer quality, it is the capture. A screenshot tool answers what fits on one screen, and on a split-pane assessment the statement, the constraints and the examples almost never fit together. OfferPilot AI handles that with a scroll-aware capture: press Ctrl+Alt+S, drag a box, you get about three seconds to scroll, drag again, repeat, then one Enter sends every shot together as a single problem. It runs on Windows 10 and 11 only, and Pro is $39 a month.

One caveat before the detail. Interview Coder ships new builds, and whether a given version lets you queue a second shot is exactly the sort of thing that changes between releases, so check their current documentation rather than trusting a comparison page, this one included. What follows is what the one-screenshot-at-a-time pattern costs you on a real problem, and how a capture designed for scrolling handles the same thing.

The problem that does not fit on one screen

Take a typical assessment layout: browser at 1080p, problem in the left pane, editor on the right. The visible description area is roughly 600 pixels tall. A mid-difficulty problem lays out like this.

  • Scroll one: title, two paragraphs of statement, a small diagram.
  • Scroll two: Example 1 and Example 2, each with input, output and an explanation block.
  • Scroll three: Example 3, the constraints (1 <= n <= 10^5, values up to 10^9), and a one-line follow-up: can you do it in O(n log n)?

Everything that decides the answer sits on scroll three. An n of 10^5 kills the quadratic solution. Values up to 10^9 kill any counting or bucket approach and push you towards coordinate compression or a heap. The follow-up line tells you which solution the grader actually wants. Screenshot the top of that pane and you get a beautifully explained brute force that passes the two examples you sent and fails the hidden tests, and you do not find out until you have spent eight minutes typing it.

The Ctrl+Alt+S flow, step by step

Press Ctrl+Alt+S

The capture arms. Nothing is sent yet, and nothing appears in a screen share either, because the overlay is excluded from screen capture at the operating system level on every plan, free included.

Drag a box round scroll one

Drag over the statement and the diagram only. A region drag rather than a full-screen grab keeps the editor, your file tree and your browser tabs out of the image, so the model reads the problem instead of your desktop.

Scroll while the window is open

You get about three seconds after each drag to scroll the pane. Two flicks of the wheel is enough. This is the step that has no equivalent in a one-shot workflow: the capture stays alive between shots instead of closing and sending.

Drag again, and again

Box the examples, scroll, then box the constraints and the follow-up line. Three drags with a scroll between each runs about twelve to fifteen seconds start to finish. Overlap the boxes slightly so no line falls in a gap between shots.

Enter sends all of them as one problem

Every shot goes in a single send, so the model reads statement, examples and constraints as one document rather than three unrelated images. Esc cancels the whole capture and sends nothing, which is what you want when the interviewer starts talking or you dragged the wrong region.

OCR runs server-side on those shots, so text-only models can read the problem as well as vision ones. That is why the same capture can be answered by more than one model at once: Grok, DeepSeek and Gemini each answer once, in parallel, and switching between them is a display toggle that re-asks nothing and costs no extra time. For LeetCode-style work, read Gemini first. What comes back is the intuition, an optimal solution, a dry run over one of the examples you captured, and the Big-O for time and space.

Where this is the wrong tool

Three honest limits, and the first is disqualifying for a lot of people.

  • Windows 10 and 11 only. No macOS build, no Linux, no mobile, no browser extension. Interview Coder is generally reported as macOS-first, and if you interview on a Mac then no feature comparison helps, because this will not launch.
  • The screenshots leave your machine. Interview audio is transcribed on-device with Whisper, but the OCR for the coding screen runs server-side. If the problem in front of you is confidential company material rather than a public-style algorithm question, that is a real consideration, and the right answer is to not capture it.
  • Capture exclusion hides the overlay from screen sharing and screen recording, and nothing else. It does not defeat a webcam, a lockdown browser, an in-person proctor, eye tracking, or a phone propped up beside your desk. If a round is monitored under rules that forbid assistance, this is the wrong tool and the wrong moment for it. Practice runs, preparation, and rounds where live help is acceptable are what the capture flow is built for.

There is a smaller practical failure mode too. On a pane with a sticky header, the header repeats in every shot, which is harmless but noisy. On one that lazy-loads images, a diagram can still be blank when your drag lands, so scroll, glance, then drag rather than dragging on muscle memory.

Test it on a problem you have already solved

The quickest way to judge any of this is not a comparison table. Open a three-scroll problem whose answer you already know, capture it in three drags, and check whether the complexity that comes back matches the constraint you captured last. If it does, the stitching worked. The free plan covers that test with no card required.
