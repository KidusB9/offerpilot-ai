Is a Karat interview recorded, and who ends up watching it?

2026 · ~7 min read · OfferPilot AI

Yes. A Karat interview is recorded, and you agree to it on a consent screen before the session opens. The recording is not simply a video of your face. It captures the call audio from both sides, the video tiles, and a replayable timeline of Karat's own code editor, so someone days later can scrub through how your solution actually appeared. Recording runs whether or not your camera is on, and the person interviewing you is not the person who decides whether you are hired.

The four channels a session captures

Karat runs the whole thing inside its own browser platform. There is no separate Zoom link and, in a standard round, no request to share your desktop. That matters for what "recorded" means here, because the platform is capturing its own page rather than scraping your computer.

  • Audio, both directions. Your explanation, the interviewer's prompts, the follow-up questions and your pauses. This channel carries most of the evaluation. A Karat round is scored heavily on how you reason out loud, so silence is not neutral, it is an empty stretch of tape.
  • Video tiles. Your webcam feed if it is running, and the interviewer's. The channel candidates fixate on, and usually the one that matters least.
  • The code editor. Karat's in-browser editor is part of the recorded session, not a snapshot taken at the end. A reviewer can watch the solution take shape, including the version you deleted at minute 22 and the debugging pass that fixed the off-by-one.
  • Structured artifacts. Rubric scores, the Interview Engineer's notes, timestamps against each question. Typed during and just after the call, and in practice the first thing a recruiter reads.

What is not captured, in a normal round, is your desktop. For pixels from outside its own tab, a browser has to draw its "Choose what to share" picker, and that dialog cannot be suppressed or missed. No picker, no desktop capture. Client setups vary, so if you are shown one, take it at face value.

One session, traced from consent screen to hiring decision

You accept the recording notice

Before the room opens you pass a screen covering recording and data handling. Read it rather than clicking through, because it is the one place your session's specific terms are stated. Declining generally means the interview cannot proceed, so treat it as a disclosure, not a negotiation.

An Interview Engineer joins and watches live

Karat's model is that a contracted engineer, not the hiring company, conducts the interview. They work from that client's question set and scoring rubric, and they are scoring in the moment while you talk. The typing you hear is normal. It is the rubric being filled in, not a verdict being drafted.

They write up immediately afterwards

Scores and notes are completed shortly after the call ends, while the session is fresh. This is why a strong recovery late in the hour still counts. The write-up describes an hour, not a first impression.

Karat reviews its own interviewers

Consistency across interviewers is the product Karat sells, so sessions are reviewed internally as a quality check. Your recording can be watched by someone with no stake in your application at all, purely to audit how the interview was run.

The client receives a report, usually within a day or two

The hiring company gets the structured report, and in most Karat programmes access to the recording alongside it. Turnaround on Karat's side is short. What happens next is the client's pipeline, not Karat's, which is why the wait after a Karat round is so often a week of nothing.

Someone at the company opens it, days later

A recruiter reads the summary. An engineering manager may skim the notes. Occasionally an engineer opens the video and jumps to one question, usually when the scores sit near the boundary and someone wants to judge the reasoning directly.

Who sees what, and when

What your interviewer is told about you beforehand

Less than you would assume, and this is deliberate. Karat's pitch to clients is a structured, repeatable interview, which means the Interview Engineer is generally working from the question set and the rubric rather than from your background. In most programmes they do not have your CV, your current employer, your years of experience or how you were sourced. They are frequently a working engineer contracted by Karat, interviewing on behalf of a company they do not work for.

Three consequences follow, and they change how you should use the hour.

  • Context you do not supply does not exist. If your strongest work is a distributed system you built at a previous job, nobody in that room knows it unless you say it. There is no shared background to lean on.
  • Rapport earns you very little. The friendly exchange at the start is real, but the score is a rubric. Warmth does not move a box measuring whether you stated a complexity bound.
  • "How did I do?" gets a non-answer, and that is not a signal. The interviewer does not make the decision and usually cannot share the outcome. A polite deflection at the end means the process worked as designed.

The corollary is that Karat rounds reward saying the quiet part out loud. State the approach before you type. Name the complexity without being asked. Say which edge case you are handling and why. On a recording scored against a checklist, articulated reasoning is the artifact.

Camera off changes almost nothing

Switching the webcam off does not stop the recording. It changes one tile of the playback while the audio, the editor timeline and the timestamps carry on exactly as before, and those channels hold most of the evaluation. A blank tile is also conspicuous to whoever opens the video later, so it draws attention rather than deflecting it. If you have a real reason, a poor connection, a shared room, an accessibility need, say so at the start and it becomes an unremarkable note rather than an unexplained gap.

Where OfferPilot AI is the wrong tool

Our overlay is excluded from screen capture at the operating system level, so it does not appear in a screen share or recording. On a Karat round that property is close to irrelevant, and it would be dishonest to imply otherwise.

  • A live human is watching. An Interview Engineer is present for the whole hour. Capture exclusion has no effect on a person looking at your webcam feed and your eyeline.
  • The editor timeline is recorded. Code that arrives faster than a person types is visible in playback regardless of what is or is not on screen.
  • It does not defeat webcam monitoring, a lockdown browser, eye tracking or a second-device camera. Those are separate systems, and screen-capture exclusion does not touch any of them.
  • It is Windows 10 and 11 only. No macOS, no Linux, no mobile, no browser extension.

The short version

Recorded: yes, from the consent screen onward, camera or no camera. Watched live: by a contracted Interview Engineer who does not know your background and does not decide your fate. Watched later: by a recruiter reading the report, sometimes by an engineer opening the video days afterwards. The channel doing the most work is the one nobody worries about, which is you, talking, while you write the code.
