Yoodli alternative: pair delivery drills with a company Q&A bank

2026 · ~6 min read · OfferPilot AI

Yoodli is a speech coach: it listens to you and reports pace, filler words and how long you talked. A live copilot does the inverse, listening to the interviewer and drafting an answer from your resume and a company-specific question bank. So the question is not which product wins, it is which sentence describes your last interview: "I rambled and said um forty times", or "they asked something only that company asks and I went silent for six seconds". The plan below runs both halves across fourteen days, and OfferPilot AI has no delivery scoring at all, so if the first sentence is yours, buy a coach instead.

Settle it tonight. Record a 90-second answer on your phone, transcribe it, take three numbers: fillers per minute, words per minute, total seconds. Clean numbers plus a six-second stall means the coaching aisle is the wrong aisle.

Fourteen days with both halves running

The halves collide deliberately in week two, and that collision names your real problem.

Days 14 and 13: baseline, fix nothing

Record three answers cold, no notes: walk me through your background, a conflict story, why this company. Log the three numbers and mark any question where you stalled before speaking. Improve nothing yet, because scoring an unwritten answer only tells you it is unwritten.

Days 12 to 9: the pause substitution drill

One story per session, four takes each, looking only at the filler count. The mechanism is replacement, not suppression: when you feel "um" arriving, close your mouth and count one. A silent beat sounds deliberate on a call; the filler does not. Fillers cluster where content is thin, so if take four still spikes in one clause, that clause is underwritten.

Days 12 to 9, second pass: pace and the 105-second skeleton

Words per minute is arithmetic you can do yourself: 220 words in 100 seconds is 132 wpm. Compare the cold take against the fourth; whichever direction it moves is your tell. Then time the shape: 15 seconds of situation, 10 of task, 60 of action, 20 of result. Action past 60 means you are narrating events rather than naming decisions.

Days 8 to 6: build the bank and mark it

Generate a bank for the exact company and role from your own resume with the Q&A generator, then do the part people skip: mark every question R, K or B. Rehearsed out loud, know the content but never said it, or blank. The B list is the whole reason this half exists.

Days 5 to 3: cross the halves

Shuffle the B list, pull six at random, answer them cold with the recorder running under the same three numbers. This is the diagnostic. Correct content at 190 wpm is a drills problem. A beautifully paced 40 seconds of nothing is a bank problem. Most people have both, on different questions.

Days 2 and 1: rig test, then taper

Test call with a friend, share your screen, confirm what they see. Headphones in, check audio still routes, since a copilot reading system playback hears the call rather than the room. Day zero is not for new material: two warm reps of your strongest story.

What the bank contains that generic reps never produce

Take a senior backend role at a payments company and a resume showing four years of Django and one monolith split. Generic practice gives you "tell me about a conflict" for the tenth time. A bank crossed against that company, role and resume asks what an idempotency key protects when a charge is retried, how you would reconcile a partial refund that failed halfway, and why the monolith split took the shape your CV claims. Those produce the six-second silence.

An honest caveat: a generator works from public inputs and your resume, not from inside knowledge of an unpublished loop. Treat it as a well-aimed superset and cut the irrelevant third rather than rehearsing it. The general question library is the wider net before you have a target company.

Where the two halves fight each other

This is the part vendor pages leave out. Reading a drafted answer off a screen damages the exact numbers you spent a fortnight improving: pace climbs, intonation flattens into recital, fillers fill the gaps while your eyes find the next line. Twelve days of drills, undone in one answer.

So they work in different weeks, not the same minute. For the six stories you drilled, deliver from memory and leave the screen unused. For a question off the B list, take the first clause and the shape of the argument, then look away and build the middle yourself. Holding P ghosts the overlay when a glance is all you want. Reading verbatim is itself a delivery problem, and no copilot fixes that.

When the coach is the right purchase and this one is not

  • Delivery is your only weakness. No filler counter, no pace metric, no tape to rewatch. That category is not matched here, it is absent.
  • One-way recorded video interviews. Nobody speaks to you, so a copilot has nothing to hear. The exercise is delivery to a camera, which is what a coach is for.
  • You are on a Mac, or practise on your phone. Windows 10 and 11 only, no web version standing in, because the capture exclusion depends on Windows compositing. Coaches run anywhere, including on a bus.
  • You want a cheap trial. The free tier is 5 minutes of listening a day, a screen-share test rather than a free interview. Tiers are on the pricing page.

What the hidden overlay covers, and what it does not

Capture exclusion means the overlay is composited out of screen sharing and screen recording at operating system level. Zoom, Teams, Meet, OBS and a plain screenshot show your desktop without it, while it stays on your own monitor. That is the entire claim. It does not defeat a webcam, a lockdown browser, an in-person invigilator, eye tracking, or a phone filming from the side of the desk. Where a process forbids outside help, no software makes it permitted, one more argument for the drills half: preparation works under every rule.
