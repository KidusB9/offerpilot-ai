What can an interviewer see when you share your screen on Zoom?

2026 · ~7 min read · OfferPilot AI

Exactly one thing decides it: which tile you clicked in the Share Screen picker. Pick a screen and you send that entire display, taskbar badges, notification toasts, the Alt-Tab thumbnail strip and any dialog that jumps to the front included. Pick an application window and Zoom sends that window's rectangle and nothing else, so a toast landing on top of it is on your monitor only. Pick a Chrome tab, which is what you get when you join Zoom in a browser rather than the desktop client, and you send the page viewport alone: no address bar, no tab titles, no bookmarks, no downloads. Row by row, below.

The three modes, side by side

Every cell describes what the interviewer receives, not what your own monitor shows. Those two diverge most in the window column, which is where the surprises live.

Two rows deserve a sentence more. Window-on-top is the one people misremember in both directions: Windows window capture reads the shared window's own pixels rather than the screen region it occupies, so covering it changes nothing in the stream. Older graphics paths could render the covered area black instead, so confirm it on your own machine rather than taking it on trust. Zoom also filters its own client windows out of a display capture, which is why a Zoom share does not produce the hall of mirrors a browser-based one does.

A fourth mode behaves unlike the other three. Share Screen, Advanced, Portion of Screen transmits a resizable rectangle you drag over your desktop. Anything that moves under it goes out, a toast that lands inside it included, so treat it as a framing tool and not a privacy tool.

Five minutes before the call, on Windows 11

Turn on Do Not Disturb, then read the automatic rules

Settings, System, Notifications, then toggle Do not disturb on. Expand Turn on do not disturb automatically and actually read it. The rule people assume is protecting them is "When duplicating my display", and a normal Zoom share on an extended desktop does not trigger it. Open Set priority notifications too, because Do Not Disturb still lets everything on that list through.

Tick Zoom's own switch

Zoom Settings, Share Screen, then Silence system notifications when sharing desktop. It is a second layer over Windows Do Not Disturb and it applies to display shares. On the same page, set Window size when screen sharing to Maintain current size so nothing leaps to fullscreen the moment you start, and leave the green border indicator enabled.

Clear the surfaces a toast leaves behind

Silencing the popup does not silence the badge. Quit or sign out of the chat and mail apps rather than muting them, so no unread count builds on the taskbar while you talk. If you will share a browser window, open a fresh profile or a guest window, because personal bookmarks sit inside that rectangle. And in Windows 11 the Widgets button opens its board on hover, not on click.

Share, then read the green border before you speak

Zoom draws a green border around precisely the surface it is encoding. If it hugs one window, only that window is going out. If it frames a whole panel, everything on that panel is going out. If it frames the panel you did not mean, stop and reshare. It is the only in-the-moment check that is actually reliable, because the tile numbers in the picker come from Zoom's own display enumeration and need not match the numbers Windows shows.

Verify from a second device, once

Start a meeting with yourself, join from your phone as a muted second participant, share each way and watch the phone. Do it once, note what surprised you, and you never have to guess again.

What they can infer even when the pixels are clean

Hiding a surface is not the same as hiding that something happened on it, and an interviewer watching a share picks up more than the frame. The pointer that slides off one edge and does not come back for eight seconds. The long static stretch while you are clearly doing something, which is the window-share signature described above. A cursor that jumps to a new position instead of travelling, which is what happens when you act on an unshared panel and come back. None of these prove anything. All of them get noticed.

Where this stops being useful

This page is about a video call between two people, not a proctored assessment, and that distinction matters more than anything above. A screen share is a stream you started and can stop. A proctored exam is software with permission to inspect your machine, count your displays, watch your webcam and log what you do. The rules there belong to whoever sent you the link.

The short version

  • Window share is the default answer for an interview. Toasts, the taskbar, Alt-Tab and other apps' dialogs all fall outside it, with no configuration needed.
  • Whole-screen share means the whole screen. If a round forces it, run the notification routine first rather than trusting muscle memory.
  • The green border is the only live source of truth. Screen 1 is Zoom's internal number, not a place on your desk.
  • A frozen picture is usually a secure desktop or a minimised window, not a dropped connection. Say so out loud and the pause stops being awkward.
